Contrapunto alla mente

Taneyev’s theory of invertible counterpoint, explained simply, with examples you can see and hear. The goal is to learn to write counterpoint in the strict style, and eventually to improvise it.

The rules

What a shift does to every interval, in two voices and in three.

  • Taneyev consonance charts: pick a vertical shift (Jv) and see what every interval becomes, with Taneyev’s suspension rules. Also: How to read the 3D models
  • Three-voice shifts: split the outer voices’ Jv for a middle voice, and see all three pair charts.

Canons

The leading voice, and a canon written over a given melody.

  • Canon proposta intervals: which melodic intervals the leading voice may use at each interval of entrance, with a checker for your own proposta. Also: Handshapes
  • Canon over a cantus firmus: plan a two-voice canon over a given melody with Taneyev’s imaginary cantus, write it on the staff, and have every bar checked. Also: Bathe’s table (c. 1596)

Notebook

Worked examples from handwritten notebooks, notated and played back.

  • Notebook: canons over a cantus firmus and invertible counterpoint, with the imaginary cantus in gray.

Drills

At the keyboard, with live feedback. These work, but they are still being worked on.

  • Move chords In progress: play every note the proposta may move to next, as one chord, as fast as you can.
  • Lead the canon In progress: make up the proposta and hear the site answer it as the risposta, each note checked as it sounds.
  • Follow the leader In progress: the site leads and you play or sing the risposta.
  • Continue the proposta In progress: finish the proposta of one of your own notebook canons, note by note in its rhythm, with the risposta copying you.
