Taneyev consonance charts

Each chart shows what every interval of the original becomes when the voices shift by Jv, and the suspension rules that follow. Intervals are zero-indexed (unison 0, octave 7, 12th 11). The charts are generated from the same rules code the checker will use. The Jv = −11 chart matches Movable Counterpoint 2 (frame 0071).

Jv

Colour of the bottom numeral

  • 2Fixed consonance: consonant in both versions
  • 5Variable consonance: becomes dissonant
  • 6Variable dissonance: becomes consonant
  • 1Fixed dissonance
  • 4Bold: perfect in either version, so no parallel or similar motion into it

Suspension marks

  • (—)Can’t form a suspension
  • —On a downbeat it must be a suspension (prepared, resolving down by step)
  • — xIts resolution is dissonant: move the free voice, or treat the resolution as a passing or neighbour tone (§§152, 167)
  • ---If suspended, its resolution is consonant and free

Reading a column

  • A mark above the numeral applies when the upper voice is suspended, a mark below when the lower voice is.
  • Grey rows show the working: the original’s marks, and the derivative’s marks, flipped where the voices cross.
  • The bottom row is the rule to follow when writing the original.

The 4th counts as a dissonance, as in two-voice writing. Between upper voices over a consonant bass it can act as an imperfect consonance (Movable Counterpoint 4, 10:23). Sources: Taneyev §§81–82, 90, 101, 128, 135; Segall, Music Theory Online 20.3.
