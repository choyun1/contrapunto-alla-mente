Canon proposta intervals

In a two-voice canon the risposta plays what the proposta played one delay ago, moved by the interval of entrance. So every melodic interval the proposta makes across the delay turns into a harmonic interval between the voices. Pick an interval of entrance to see which melodic intervals stay consonant, with a short first-species canon to see and hear. Any Jv from −21 to +21 can be picked; the common ones are starred, as on the consonance charts page. Intervals are zero-indexed (step 1, 3rd 2, octave 7).

Worked examples in the notebook: two-part canons and infinite canons.

span
The proposta’s melodic interval from a note to the note one delay later. Positive: rising. With a delay of one measure in whole notes, that is from one note to the next; with two measures, from one note to the note after next.
Jv
The interval of entrance, in steps. Positive: the risposta is above.
n
The interval the risposta forms against the proposta: n = Jv − span. This is Taneyev’s n = m + Jv, with the canon at the unison as the original (m = −span). A negative n means the risposta is below.

Verdict

  • freeImperfect consonance: use it freely
  • perfectPerfect consonance: not twice in a row while both voices move
  • susp.Dissonance: on a strong beat only as a prepared suspension

Suspension marks

  • —This voice may be the suspended one, resolving down by step
  • (—)This voice can’t be suspended here
  • A suspension in the risposta means the proposta was suspended one delay earlier.

Check a proposta

Write the proposta as pitch names in equal note values, one per time unit (C4 is middle C). The delay is counted in the same units. Every note is treated as a strong beat, as in a whole-note framework.

For a quick way to remember these moves at the keyboard, see Handshapes. To write a canon over a given melody, see Canon over a cantus firmus.

The 4th counts as a dissonance, as in two-voice writing. Between upper voices over a consonant bass it can act as an imperfect consonance (Movable Counterpoint 4, 10:23). Steps are diatonic, so a tritone counts as its diatonic size. These rules are derived from the rules core, not yet checked against a § of Taneyev’s Doctrine of the Canon.
