Canon over a cantus firmus

To write a two-voice canon over a given melody, don’t guess. Write it against an imaginary voice first (Taneyev’s method, as taught in Movable Counterpoint 4), then write it here and have every bar checked.

Worked examples in the notebook: canons over a cantus firmus, with the imaginary cantus.

  1. Choose the setup: where the cantus (CF), proposta (P) and risposta (R) sit from top to bottom, the interval of entrance and the delay.
  2. Place the imaginary cantus firmus (I.C.F.): copy the cantus, moved the opposite way to the canon (by the interval of entrance, and earlier by the delay). Whatever the proposta forms against this copy, the risposta will form against the real cantus a delay later.
  3. Read the guide intervals. The interval between the two copies in each bar is the vertical shift Jv the proposta must be written in there: a unison is simple counterpoint, an octave, 10th or 12th is textbook invertible counterpoint, and a 2nd or 7th leaves almost no notes. Parallel 2nds or 7ths mean the setup won’t work, so change it.
  4. Write the proposta one bar at a time, consonant with both the cantus and the imaginary cantus; the risposta copies it and all three sounding voices are checked together. Each bar is a two-voice shift chart (see the consonance charts), and the three voices follow the three-voice shifts page: a 4th is fine between two upper voices over a consonant bass.
  5. Elaborate the whole-note framework with faster notes, suspensions and passing tones, and relax the imitation near the cadence.

The default is Fux’s Dorian cantus in the alto, with the risposta a 12th above the proposta. At one bar, as in the video, bar 4 has a 9th guide (Jv = −8) and needs a suspension, so no whole-note framework exists; at three bars one does. Pitches are diatonic (no sharps or flats), one note per bar: transpose a hymn tune to C major or a church mode first.

Setup

No whole-note framework keeps every rule with this setup: some bar needs a suspension or a decorated line, as in the video. Write your own here, or work backwards and find a setup that fits.
